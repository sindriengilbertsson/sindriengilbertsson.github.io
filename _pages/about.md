I am a PhD candidate in Economics at the University of Amsterdam and the <a href="https://tinbergen.nl/home" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Tinbergen Institute</a>, specialising in industrial organisation and competition policy. My supervisors are <a href="https://www.uva.nl/profiel/o/n/a.m.onderstal/a.m.onderstal.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Sander Onderstal</a> and <a href="https://www.uva.nl/profiel/s/c/m.p.schinkel/m.p.schinkel.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Maarten Pieter Schinkel</a>. My research examines cartel enforcement, sustainability cooperation, worker mobility restrictions and market entry.

While my research covers a broad range of topics, the core question always concerns (imperfect) competition. My interest in competition policy began while working at the Icelandic Competition Authority after completing my bachelor degree. I enjoyed applying economic analysis to practical questions that affect people's everyday lives, and that experience motivated me to pursue a PhD in the field. In 2025, I spent a year at the OECD's Directorate for Employment, Labour and Social Affairs (ELS), working on post-employment restrictions in labour contracts, particularly on <a href="https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-regulation-of-non-compete-clauses-across-oecd-countries_88e3eb6e-en.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">non-compete clauses</a> and their effects on workers and societal welfare. I continue to work on these questions with colleagues at the OECD.
